# Continental Bio

Continental Bio is a fictional Cambridge, Massachusetts biopharmaceutical company. This file is classroom canon for a Chief of Staff exercise. Nothing here describes a real company, a real drug, or real people.

## Company

Continental Bio discovers and develops biologic medicines for neurological disease. The company was founded in 2014 by Helen Williamson and Dr. Priya Raman. Headquarters are at 450 Kendall Street, Cambridge, MA. About 310 people work there, plus a small clinical-operations group in Raleigh.

The science focus is large-molecule and nucleic-acid approaches to neurodegeneration: monoclonal antibodies, antisense oligonucleotides, and a neuroinflammation biologic. The company is past the venture-story stage. It has revenue from a single major transaction and a remaining pipeline it still has to prove.

Mission: slow or prevent neurological disease with biologic medicines that can be manufactured, regulated, and paid for. The internal shorthand is "make the hard biology manufacturable."

## The GSK transaction

In March 2026 Continental Bio sold its lead anti-amyloid plaque antibody, **CB-101 (lexabamab)**, to GSK. The medicine is aimed at Alzheimer's-related plaque. GSK paid $1.15 billion upfront. Up to $900 million in development and commercial milestones remains, plus a mid-single-digit royalty on net sales. GSK owns the program. Continental Bio is running a transition-services agreement through 31 December 2026: tech transfer, batch records, the phase 2 clinical database, and a handful of contractor assignments.

The sale did not make the company finished. Cash is real, the earn-out is not, and the rest of the pipeline is earlier and riskier. Helen's job in the second half of 2026 is to keep the GSK relationship clean, decide what the company is now, and not let the remaining programs starve or sprawl.

## Remaining pipeline

- **CB-201 (rivastide)** — antisense oligonucleotide for a genetic form of ALS. Phase 1 ongoing. Manufacturing slot at Harbor Biologics is the constraint everyone argues about.
- **CB-214** — monoclonal antibody against extracellular alpha-synuclein for Parkinson's disease. Preclinical. A pre-IND meeting with FDA is on the calendar for the autumn.
- **CB-230** — neuroinflammation biologic aimed at microglial activation. Discovery. Priya wants to keep it. Marcus wants a kill-or-partner decision before year end.

CB-101 is no longer Continental Bio's to develop. People still say its name constantly, because the transition, the royalty, and the reputation all sit on it.

## Helen Williamson

Helen Williamson is Chief Executive Officer. She is 54. She trained as a chemical engineer, spent a decade in biologics manufacturing at larger companies, then co-founded Continental Bio. She reads the operational detail herself: batch records, contract redlines, and the cash forecast. She does not like surprises in board pre-reads, and she does not like a meeting that could have been a two-paragraph note.

Working day: about 08:00–18:30 America/New_York, Monday to Friday, with email that leaks into evenings. She keeps 4 to 6 hours of meetings and protects the rest for reading. Her assistant is Nate Okonkwo. Nate is not part of the close executive cohort below. He schedules, chases, and forwards.

Email: helen.williamson@continentalbio.example

## Close cohort

These six people are Helen's operating circle. Network questions about "who she actually works with" should land here. Together with Helen they are the internal set.

### Dr. Priya Raman — Chief Scientific Officer

Co-founder. Runs research and the scientific case for CB-201, CB-214, and CB-230. She will fight to keep CB-230 alive. She writes long emails and short meeting agendas. priya.raman@continentalbio.example

### Marcus Hale — Chief Financial Officer

Joined in 2021 from a public biotech. Owns cash, the GSK earn-out model, royalty accounting, and what "runway" means after a windfall. He wants a portfolio decision before the January board. marcus.hale@continentalbio.example

### Dr. Elena Voss — Chief Medical Officer

Owns clinical development. CB-201 phase 1 is hers. She is careful in writing and blunt in person. She is the person FDA will remember. elena.voss@continentalbio.example

### Jonathan Park — General Counsel

Owns the GSK contract, the TSA, disclosure, and employment issues after the sale. He flags loose language. He would rather delay a note than send a sloppy one. jonathan.park@continentalbio.example

### Amira Solano — Head of Corporate Development

Ran the GSK process and still owns the relationship day to day. She is the person Claire Dumont at GSK calls first. She wants the TSA to end cleanly and the milestone definitions to stay unambiguous. amira.solano@continentalbio.example

### Dr. Kenji Okada — Head of Regulatory Affairs

Owns FDA and EMA strategy for the remaining pipeline. He is preparing the CB-214 pre-IND. He worries that GSK questions about CB-101 will eat the regulatory group's calendar. kenji.okada@continentalbio.example

## Threads that keep coming back

1. GSK TSA and tech transfer. What is still Continental Bio's job, and what is GSK's.
2. Milestone and royalty language. Marcus and Jonathan do not fully agree on when a milestone is "earned."
3. What the company is after CB-101. Platform, or two clinical assets and a discovery project.
4. CB-201 manufacturing slot at Harbor. Miss it and phase 1 slips.
5. CB-214 pre-IND. Kenji needs Helen's time and Elena's clinical judgment.
6. Whether CB-230 gets a 2027 budget.
7. Hiring and retention after a windfall. People are tired and a few are looking.
8. Board pack for the late-October meeting. Robert wants decisions, not updates.

## How Helen writes

Short paragraphs. She names the decision and the date. She copies the smallest group that can act. She rarely uses exclamation points. When she is annoyed it shows up as a one-line question, not a speech.
